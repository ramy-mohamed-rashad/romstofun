Tekken 3 pushed the PSX hardware to its peak performance thresholds, utilizing rapid sprite manipulation, complex hit-box calculations, and bone-crunching martial arts animation routines that defined competitive fighting in its era.

3. Known Graphical Fixes & Emulation Patches

Original 32-Bit Polygon Warping & Texture Wobble

The Issue: The original PlayStation 1 GPU lacked sub-pixel precision and perspective-correct texture mapping, causing 3D geometry to visibly warp and jitter as the camera shifts.

The Fix: In DuckStation Enhancements, toggle "PGXP (Precision Geometry Transform)" ON. Enable both "Perspective Correct Textures" and "Culling Correction". 3D geometry will render as stable and crisp as modern PC ports!

16-Bit Color Banding on Backgrounds

The Issue: Original PS1 hardware rendered at 16-bit color depth, creating harsh gradient stepped bands across skies and dark corridors.

The Fix: Enable "True Color Rendering (24-bit RGB)" in DuckStation. This renders clean, smooth color gradients across all 3D assets and backdrops.

4. Touch Controls vs. Bluetooth Gamepads

Because Tekken 3 relies heavily on frame-precise input execution, multi-button simultaneous presses, and rapid directional motions (quarter-circle rolls, dragon-punch inputs), virtual on-screen controls can occasionally lead to misregistered inputs during intense combo exchanges. For casual arcade ladders, expanding the virtual button touch radii and configuring translucent 45% opacity overlays provides an accessible experience.

To truly dominate competitive matches, pairing a physical low-latency controller such as the Razer Kishi V2, GameSir G8 Galileo, or a Sony DualSense gamepad via USB-C or Bluetooth is strongly recommended. Physical microswitch D-pads and tactile face buttons guarantee zero dropped inputs, microsecond-accurate parries, and immediate combo execution.
